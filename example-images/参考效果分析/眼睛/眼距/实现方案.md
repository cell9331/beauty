# Eye spacing: candidate field

**Status: proposed mathematics only.** This does not identify proprietary internals, implement the SDK, or qualify reproduced appearance. Read the [evidence chain](分析链路.md) and [shared definitions, derivatives, safety and sampling](../实现公共约定.md).

## Minimum geometry and source domain

Use separately calibrated temporal translation plateaus with continuous periocular support. A shared normalized response is a later hypothesis; the two measured pixel amplitudes are not forced equal. Freeze reliable source eye geometry: center/reference c, positive scale L, nasal-to-temporal tangent e_t and inferior normal e_n. The common definitions provide t,n and all normalization derivatives. For this pair s=0 is the observed left-endpoint image and s=1 the right-endpoint mapping, not a neutral-to-positive control. Set the candidate response to zero at s=0 and fit its endpoint value; choosing a linear response between them would be an unmeasured design choice.

## Forward map and full Jacobian

```text
r_i(p,s) = L_i a_i(s) e_(t,i)
D(p,s) = sum_i w_i(p) r_i(p,s)
J_F = I + sum_i r_i (grad_(w_i))^T
```

Use the common compact support/protection product and its complete derivative. Smoothstep is only one possible design kernel. The measured optical-flow window and a binary eye/iris mask do not define w. Fit any superior/inferior or nasal/temporal asymmetry from the source-matched transition, not from the control name. If geometry or directions vary spatially rather than remaining frozen constants, their derivatives also enter J_r.

## Calibration, scope and protection

e_t is nasal-to-temporal for each eye, so positive a moves both cores apart. Fit each endpoint plateau against independent correspondences and divide by a reliable source L_i only when that geometry is measured. The observed −26.96/+25.58 px spans do not define a UI center or equal/opposite one-sided strengths. If support overlaps on the bridge, fit and audit the combined field, not each contribution in isolation.

The full iris/lid core translates. The nasal bridge, nose and brows need explicit smooth protection and separate observed texture checks. Do not freeze only the iris while moving the eyelids, and do not silently normalize overlapping weights, which would alter the measured core response.

The compact support, bridge-side overlap/protection, bilateral amplitude relation and response between endpoints remain unmeasured. Farneback agrees well in the core, but retained moving-point disagreement has a P99 tail of 4.77 px; a good median does not qualify every transition. More independent nasal/temporal boundary texture is needed to fit falloff.

## Future evaluation sequence

1. Admit reliable source geometry and ownership; retain identity on degenerate or uncertain inputs.
2. Fit the internal model with independent moving correspondences, then determine support using separate decay/protection observations. Preserve accepted outliers and residual structure.
3. Evaluate the complete Jacobian and any multi-effect overlap; establish a valid inverse/coverage strategy. A safe core or positive sampled determinant alone is insufficient.
4. Solve q=F_s(p), check the inverse residual, and sample the original source once. Use the common explicit identity/failure result for unsafe support, failed inverse or uncovered targets.
5. Compare output contours, texture and protected regions against the actual endpoint mapping on the same source points.

Require the two outward core signs, nearly preserved intra-eye distances, the measured relative separation increase and stationary bridge/brow texture. A model that stretches one eye to achieve the correct inter-eye distance or moves the bridge fails the mechanism check.

The zero/translation/expansion/rotation controls reported at the [eye index](../README.md) qualify the measuring method only on their own synthetic cases. No actual candidate output has been rendered or accepted in this task.

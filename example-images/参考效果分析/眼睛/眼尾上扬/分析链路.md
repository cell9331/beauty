# Outer-eye lift: evidence and inference

Analysis date: 2026-10-16. Pair: `IMG_1943.PNG → IMG_1944.PNG`. Forward displacement is source to target, +x right and +y down, in native screenshot photo-grid pixels. The [eye index](../README.md) records shared input qualification and alignment. The native common grid is retained, without local shape registration. The observations identify this endpoint span only.

## Observations that distinguish the mechanism

Native crops show the outer corners and neighboring lid contour moving upward. The inner corner and central eye texture move much less. In signed fields, dy is negative around each outer eye and decays toward the inner side and surrounding skin; dx is smaller. A local outer-region lift is therefore a simpler candidate than whole-eye translation.

The broad eye-window DIS dy P1–P99 ranges are `−17.65 to approximately 0 px` on the image-left and `−19.25 to approximately 0 px` on the image-right. Corresponding dx ranges are `−2.65 to +1.00 px` and `−1.38 to +2.62 px`. These are region percentiles of an estimate, not single-edge displacement or whole-field extrema. Broad-window magnitude medians are near zero because much of the window is outside the changing region; they must not be used to describe the moving outer corner.

Eye-window forward/backward closure P95 is 0.32/0.53 px. Farneback and DIS locate the same main upward region, but their estimated footprints differ: disagreement P95 is 5.53/5.48 px and P99 is 12.20/13.48 px. This is especially visible near outer skin and transitions. The reference identifies a localized lift more securely than it identifies the full support contour or the strongest continuous-field peak.

## Independent source-texture correspondence

The original 22 px source grid accepted 260 of 387 candidates. Rejections were 70 low-texture, 33 ambiguous, 4 reverse-ambiguous, 2 reverse-closure, 1 low-NCC, and 17 crop-edge cases. Nine accepted points moved more than 2 px and 244 were stationary below 0.5 px. DIS/NCC difference P95 was 1.22 px for the moving points and 0.10 px for stationary points. These are method differences, not errors against ground truth.

The targeted review selected 433 source-texture candidates on an 8 px grid. Search was ±64 px with boundary expansion to ±96 px; NCC, uniqueness, and reverse closure determined acceptance without DIS initialization or filtering. A wider template includes different proportions of stationary and lifted texture, so radius sensitivity is reported explicitly.

For the 5 px templates, accepted moving dy spans `−20.67 to −2.02 px`, and dx spans `−1.82 to +1.84 px`. Those extrema belong to sampled matches only. They support sizeable upward motion with a secondary horizontal term, rather than a guaranteed maximum displacement over the entire effect.

An additional post-review intersection of the 5 px and 7 px tests retained 225 positions, including 24 moving points, with target positions within 1.5 px. Moving-point DIS disagreement P95 was 0.65 px. Their dy P5–P95 was `−10.13 to −2.17 px`, showing that this more stable subset mostly samples the decay rather than every peak. The subset must not be used to erase larger accepted motions or rejected boundary observations. Neighboring templates overlap, so sample count does not imply independent statistical trials.

The inner third of each source eye had no >2 px accepted motion at any reviewed radius, with 52–64 accepted points per side/radius. That supports a relatively protected inner region in this pair. It is an observed finite sample, not proof of an exact zero-displacement anatomical endpoint.

## Inference and competing explanations

**Fact:** the outer region rises, multiple source-selected points distributed across the central/inner region are much less mobile, and the field returns toward zero outside the local region. **Hypothesis:** a compact outer-region upward bump with continuous surrounding support. **Argued against as a complete description:** one uniform eye translation. The observed spatial distribution supports locality, but it does not by itself exclude a rigidly rotating eye core with surrounding support decay. A pivot near the inner corner can leave inner texture nearly stationary while raising the outer end, without requiring opposite-sign inner motion.

A predominantly vertical bump is a low-degree-of-freedom starting candidate for the principal component, rather than a completed model-selection result against rotation. A rigid core rotation with an inner-side pivot, a locally weighted rotation, and another local warp remain competing hypotheses. This feature has not undergone a rigid fit and pairwise-distance test on the same source-defined core correspondences; without that comparison, rigid core rotation cannot be excluded. The bump's falsifier is a repeatable, independently supported horizontal pattern or pairwise geometry that it cannot reproduce. A future distinction must compare complete dx/dy predictions and pairwise geometry on the same accepted source-selected points, rather than infer rotation or its absence from dy signs alone.

The exact support shape, peak plateau versus rounded maximum, lower/upper skin asymmetry, and intermediate slider response remain unknown. More unique source texture at the outer peak and transition would distinguish them; increasing smoothness or fitting more control points cannot supply that missing evidence.

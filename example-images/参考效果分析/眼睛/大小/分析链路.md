## Observed pattern and core geometry

Both eyes expand in two dimensions: the horizontal component points away from the eye center on both sides, while the superior and inferior vertical components oppose each other. The larger horizontal peak does not, by itself, prove anisotropic scaling: the source eye is much wider than it is tall.

In source-defined central ellipses, dense affine diagonal gains are approximately 1.12–1.14 in both image directions. The 10 independent core points per eye give horizontal gains 1.13/1.11 and vertical gains 1.17/1.15. Their rigid-fit P95 residuals are 6.17/5.25 px, versus 0.61/0.58 px for an affine fit. Thus distance-changing expansion is much better supported than a rigid translation or rotation. The sparse sample and template deformation do not resolve small departures from isotropy.

## Independent verification and method limits

Primary template matching selects the strongest source texture in spatial cells without a flow seed. A 19 px template searches ±64 px, with expansion to ±96 px for a boundary peak. It checks contrast, NCC ≥0.78, a separated competitor margin ≥0.025, and independently matched reverse closure ≤1.5 px. These are analysis choices, not application parameters. 263/376 candidates pass; rejections: 68 low-texture, 25 ambiguous, 2 reverse-ambiguous, 1 reverse-closure, 17 edge. No accepted search-boundary point is treated as a measured maximum.

The remaining 8 accepted points lie between those classes. These differences are not truth errors; all accepted disagreements remain. DIS uses a fine-scale estimate; Farneback is a second dense method with a larger averaging window. Their disagreement can reflect smoothing and capture differences, particularly at sharp lid transitions. Do not average it away or interpret a smooth field as hundreds of independent skin observations.

Source-defined protection windows give independent NCC checks: brows: 58 accepted, magnitude P95 0.057 px; nose: 30 accepted, magnitude P95 0.066 px; left-cheek: 13 accepted, magnitude P95 0.108 px; right-cheek: 2 accepted, magnitude P95 0.095 px. Small counts supply correspondingly weak coverage. Distributed stable photo texture is checked independently in the shared registration audit. Near-zero dense output alone is not proof of protection.

## Fact → hypothesis → exclusions → unknowns

**Fact:** Both eyes expand in two dimensions: the horizontal component points away from the eye center on both sides, while the superior and inferior vertical components oppose each other. The larger horizontal peak does not, by itself, prove anisotropic scaling: the source eye is much wider than it is tall. **Candidate:** Start with a locally isotropic expansion core and a separately fitted continuous envelope. A distinct horizontal/vertical gain is a competing refinement only if independent residuals consistently require it.

**Excluded as sufficient explanations:** A constant translation cannot explain opposing signs around each center. A rigid map cannot explain the core distance changes and its substantially worse residual. An iris-only warp cannot explain the broad motion extending toward both canthi and periocular tissue.

**Unknown / next discriminator:** The exact reference center, support shape, plateau edge, and small anisotropy remain unidentified. Core gains are endpoint-relative fits, not neutral strengths. Additional source-selected correspondences across the upper/lower and nasal/temporal transitions would distinguish an isotropic core from an anisotropic or non-linear core.

# Analysis chain

## Question and observable scope

This analysis asks what geometric difference can be measured between the supplied photograph S0 and the single screenshot state `IMG_1938`. It does not identify an isolated low-to-high slider span. No screenshot confirms S0 as the common zero state, and one screenshot cannot establish response linearity, a negative direction, or a neutral-to-endpoint strength.

The reference label 去脂 is input metadata. The mechanism hypothesis below is derived from the observed two-dimensional movement, independently of that label. No color-effect analysis or internal closed-source algorithm claim is made.

The forward vector is `D(p) = p_target − p_source`, with x right and y down. Units are native screenshot pixels after global alignment. Fitting uses stable hair, background, side texture, and lower photographic texture outside a generous eye/brow exclusion. Independent forehead and nose windows are protection observations, not registration fitting regions.

## Input and alignment audit

All 23 eye screenshots share the same native grid. A stable-texture audit selected 153 candidates before target matching. For every screenshot, 143 candidates passed the independent NCC criteria: 95 fitting points and 48 spatially distributed holdout points. Ten candidates were rejected because their correlation peaks were ambiguous. Every accepted screenshot-to-screenshot peak was at exactly the same integer position. Zero-change and known `(dx,dy) = (3,−2)` controls recovered the expected integer vectors.

The stable region contains 1,346,150 pixels. Sixteen screenshots are byte-identical there. The remaining seven differ at only 167–168 upper-hair pixels, with a maximum channel difference of 6/255; their accepted stable texture correspondences remain at zero displacement. Therefore the screenshot pixels are retained without another resampling. This supports common grid and photographic content, not common neutral state or isolated-control status.

S0 requires a global scale and crop. Mutual ratio-tested SIFT matches on stable target texture provide 2,652 candidate correspondences. The fitting split contains 1,828 matches; RANSAC retains 1,778, with residual median 0.279 px and P95 0.610 px. The other 824 matches were held out before fitting: their median residual is 0.292 px and P95 is 0.859 px. Their maximum is 46.696 px, reflecting a false match retained in the holdout report. This tail is not hidden by filtering holdouts against the fitted transform.

An independent check selects 153 stable source-texture patches on the common grid. It accepts 131 and rejects 7 for NCC quality and 15 for ambiguity. Accepted displacement residuals have median 0.072 px, P95 0.200 px, and maximum 0.374 px. Accepted patches cover upper, lower, left, and right stable regions. Their region-wise P95 residuals are 0.168, 0.202, 0.245, and 0.095 px, respectively. These independent patch residuals support the quality of the single global alignment; they are not a universal accuracy bound for eyelid matching.

The fitted scale is approximately 0.943765 and the rotation is approximately 0.000515 degrees. S0 is resampled directly from its original pixels to the native screenshot grid once. No local or deformable alignment is used. Raw transforms and point locations remain in ignored local measurement files.

## Field measurement and independent correspondences

A forward and backward DIS field is estimated in the photographic eye neighborhood. Dense estimates reveal spatial organization; low-texture filled values are not counted as independent observations. Independently, source-texture patches are selected by spatial grids and local gradient energy, without using the DIS field to initialize, select, or reject them.

The eyelid check uses 21 px patches and an initial ±18 px search. Acceptance requires forward and reverse NCC at least 0.94, peak uniqueness at least 0.008 outside a 3 px neighborhood, no unresolved search-edge maximum, and forward/reverse closure at most 0.6 px. One upper-lid candidate reaches the initial search boundary. Expanding its search to ±36 px leads to rejection for NCC quality, rather than treating the boundary as measured motion.

## Direction, support, and protection observations

The moving broad-eye group has median `(dx,dy) = (−0.134, −1.223)` px. The upper-lid-focused moving groups give:

| Region | Moving accepted points | Median `(dx,dy)`, px | Maximum observed sparse magnitude, px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Image-left upper lid | 12 | `(0.189, −1.154)` | 1.966 |
| Image-right upper lid | 15 | `(−0.085, −1.220)` | 1.822 |

Negative dy, repeated on both sides and supported by independent patches, is the strongest directional finding. Horizontal components are smaller, but they are not proved to be exactly zero. The largest broad-eye sparse correspondence is 2.114 px; it is not the continuous field's global maximum.

Dense upper-lid magnitude maxima are approximately 2.591 and 2.407 px. Upper-lid forward/backward closure P95 values are 0.123 and 0.116 px. Dense peaks have greater estimator dependence than the independent sparse observations, so the maxima are reported separately.

Forehead and nose accepted patches have displacement P95 values of 0.217 and 0.132 px, with maxima of 0.303 and 0.234 px. None exceeds the 0.5 px audit threshold. These observations argue against a global upward image shift. They do not certify every eyebrow, iris, lash, lower-lid, or surrounding-skin pixel as fixed.

## Evidence and inference

[The evidence panel](证据/位移证据.png) shows aligned S0 and the observed state, signed dx and dy fields, and marked horizontal and vertical profile locations. Profiles are 7 px strip medians of the dense estimate. Accepted nearby NCC points are drawn separately and can be up to 12 px away from the strip center; they are not exact samples of the plotted curve. Axes retain native screenshot coordinates. Component color bars and profile ranges cover the complete estimated field range in the displayed region.

**Fact → hypothesis:** upper-lid texture moves upward by roughly 1–2 px while independent forehead and nose windows remain near the registration residual floor. A compact superior displacement bump near the upper lid is a reasonable low-complexity candidate for this source-relative appearance.

**Exclusion evidence:** the localization and protection observations are inconsistent with a global image translation. They do not establish a rigid eyelid translation, exact corner fixation, isotropic expansion, or a volume-removal mechanism. No paired-distance or rotation fit qualifies those stronger models.

**Remaining unknowns:** isolated control amplitude, certified zero state, intermediate response, exact support boundary, whether corner displacement is strictly zero, cross-photo scaling, and possible accumulated effects. An authenticated zero capture and a controlled slider sequence from the same image are needed to assign the measured source-relative difference specifically to this control. Additional reliable correspondences across upper/lower transitions would distinguish a translated plateau from a local bump and quantify the support more precisely.

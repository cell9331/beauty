## Observed pattern and core geometry

The superior eye region moves upward while the inferior region moves downward. Vertical displacement dominates, with smaller but visible horizontal components. The upper lobe is larger than the lower one in this comparison. This changes aperture height rather than simply moving the eye.

Dense eye-window dy P1–P99 spans about −11.28 to +8.00 px on image-left and −11.05 to +7.83 px on image-right. A source-defined thin core yields dense vertical affine gains about 1.51/1.50, while only 8 independent core points per eye yield 1.45/1.68 and P95 affine residuals 3.34/2.32 px. These differences and second-method sensitivity preclude a precise universal gain or pivot. They still distinguish dominant vertical expansion from translation.

## Independent verification and method limits

Primary template matching selects the strongest source texture in spatial cells without a flow seed. A 19 px template searches ±64 px, with expansion to ±96 px for a boundary peak. It checks contrast, NCC ≥0.78, a separated competitor margin ≥0.025, and independently matched reverse closure ≤1.5 px. These are analysis choices, not application parameters. 253/382 candidates pass; rejections: 72 low-texture, 27 ambiguous, 7 reverse-ambiguous, 1 low-ncc, 2 reverse-closure, 17 edge, 3 reverse-low-texture. No accepted search-boundary point is treated as a measured maximum.

The remaining 6 accepted points lie between those classes. These differences are not truth errors; all accepted disagreements remain. DIS uses a fine-scale estimate; Farneback is a second dense method with a larger averaging window. Their disagreement can reflect smoothing and capture differences, particularly at sharp lid transitions. Do not average it away or interpret a smooth field as hundreds of independent skin observations.

Source-defined protection windows give independent NCC checks: brows: 58 accepted, magnitude P95 0.057 px; nose: 30 accepted, magnitude P95 0.066 px; left-cheek: 13 accepted, magnitude P95 0.108 px; right-cheek: 2 accepted, magnitude P95 0.096 px. Small counts supply correspondingly weak coverage. Distributed stable photo texture is checked independently in the shared registration audit. Near-zero dense output alone is not proof of protection.

## Fact → hypothesis → exclusions → unknowns

**Fact:** The superior eye region moves upward while the inferior region moves downward. Vertical displacement dominates, with smaller but visible horizontal components. The upper lobe is larger than the lower one in this comparison. This changes aperture height rather than simply moving the eye. **Candidate:** Test a normal-direction expansion about a fitted source anchor line, with independently fitted surrounding support. A pure normal model is the minimum candidate; retain horizontal residuals as a challenge, rather than declaring them zero.

**Excluded as sufficient explanations:** A constant vertical translation cannot move upper/lower texture in opposite directions. The size control has appreciable horizontal expansion across the whole eye; here the normal component is dominant. A rigid rotation would also require a coherent full-vector rotational pattern and preserved pairwise distances, which is not the principal observed pattern.

**Unknown / next discriminator:** The anchor line, superior/inferior gain split, horizontal follow motion and support boundaries remain uncertain. Dense core DIS–Farneback P95 differences reach 6.62/7.27 px and forward/backward P95 1.20/0.98 px. Do not promote the fitted 1.5-scale estimate or a visually chosen fixed corner into calibration. Capture a neutral and intermediate states, then add independent upper/lower lid and iris correspondences.

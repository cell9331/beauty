## Observed pattern and core geometry

From the observed left to right endpoint, both eyes move down. The core has a nearly constant vertical displacement, with little horizontal motion; the surrounding field returns toward zero. This is a translating core, not a whole-image displacement.

Source-defined core ellipses contain 8/9 accepted independent points. Their median dy is +19.52/+18.40 px; P10–P90 is +19.11 to +19.64 / +18.10 to +18.47 px. Median dx is −0.01/−0.18 px. Rigid-fit P95 residuals are 0.37/0.52 px, and both dense and independent local affine gains are close to one. The small core and incomplete boundary coverage support approximate core translation, not perfect rigidity everywhere.

## Independent verification and method limits

Primary template matching selects the strongest source texture in spatial cells without a flow seed. A 19 px template searches ±64 px, with expansion to ±96 px for a boundary peak. It checks contrast, NCC ≥0.78, a separated competitor margin ≥0.025, and independently matched reverse closure ≤1.5 px. These are analysis choices, not application parameters. 254/382 candidates pass; rejections: 75 low-texture, 34 ambiguous, 2 reverse-ambiguous, 17 edge. No accepted search-boundary point is treated as a measured maximum.

The remaining 0 accepted points lie between those classes. These differences are not truth errors; all accepted disagreements remain. DIS uses a fine-scale estimate; Farneback is a second dense method with a larger averaging window. Their disagreement can reflect smoothing and capture differences, particularly at sharp lid transitions. Do not average it away or interpret a smooth field as hundreds of independent skin observations.

Source-defined protection windows give independent NCC checks: brows: 58 accepted, magnitude P95 0.057 px; nose: 30 accepted, magnitude P95 0.067 px; left-cheek: 13 accepted, magnitude P95 0.108 px; right-cheek: 2 accepted, magnitude P95 0.096 px. Small counts supply correspondingly weak coverage. Distributed stable photo texture is checked independently in the shared registration audit. Near-zero dense output alone is not proof of protection.

## Fact → hypothesis → exclusions → unknowns

**Fact:** From the observed left to right endpoint, both eyes move down. The core has a nearly constant vertical displacement, with little horizontal motion; the surrounding field returns toward zero. This is a translating core, not a whole-image displacement. **Candidate:** Use an inferior translation plateau with smooth source-specific superior/inferior and nasal/temporal falloff. Fit each eye independently before testing whether a shared normalized amplitude is justified.

**Excluded as sufficient explanations:** Opposite top/bottom signs expected from a pure expansion are absent in the active core. Global screenshot motion is contradicted by distributed stable texture. A vertical shear or dilation is unnecessary to explain the tightly grouped core correspondences, although the transition necessarily deforms skin.

**Unknown / next discriminator:** The left/right difference is observed; it is not automatically a bug or proof of two unrelated mechanisms. Exact plateau limits, asymmetric transition widths, reference scale normalization and intermediate response remain unknown. Boundary measurements at a second dense scale/method and independent skin texture should determine decay rather than extending a fitted core affine map through the skin.

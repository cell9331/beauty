# Eye vertical position: candidate field

**Status: proposed mathematics only.** This does not identify proprietary internals, implement the SDK, or qualify reproduced appearance. Read the [evidence chain](分析链路.md) and [shared definitions, derivatives, safety and sampling](../实现公共约定.md).

## Minimum geometry and source domain

Use an inferior translation plateau with smooth source-specific superior/inferior and nasal/temporal falloff. Fit each eye independently before testing whether a shared normalized amplitude is justified. Freeze reliable source eye geometry: center/reference c, positive scale L, nasal-to-temporal tangent e_t and inferior normal e_n. The common definitions provide t,n and all normalization derivatives. For this pair s=0 is the observed left-endpoint image and s=1 the right-endpoint mapping, not a neutral-to-positive control. Set the candidate response to zero at s=0 and fit its endpoint value; choosing a linear response between them would be an unmeasured design choice.

## Forward map and full Jacobian

```text
r(p,s) = L a(s) e_n
D(p,s) = w(p) r(p,s)
J_r = 0
J_F = I + L a(s) e_n (grad_w)^T
```

Use the common compact support/protection product and its complete derivative. Smoothstep is only one possible design kernel. The measured optical-flow window and a binary eye/iris mask do not define w. Fit any superior/inferior or nasal/temporal asymmetry from the source-matched transition, not from the control name. If geometry or directions vary spatially rather than remaining frozen constants, their derivatives also enter J_r.

## Calibration, scope and protection

Fit the plateau displacement from moving core points, then normalize by a newly measured reliable source L for each photo. The observed downward medians +19.52/+18.40 px are endpoint spans on this grid. They do not specify signed UI ranges or two equal half-amplitudes. The direction e_n is source-specific; a face rotation must rotate the field rather than keep a fixed screen-down direction.

Move the iris and both lids together inside the plateau. Return to zero before independently protected brows, bridge and remote cheek windows. Do not force the canthi to remain fixed while translating the rest of the eye. Support must be wide enough to avoid compressing the upper/lower transition.

The left/right difference is observed; it is not automatically a bug or proof of two unrelated mechanisms. Exact plateau limits, asymmetric transition widths, reference scale normalization and intermediate response remain unknown. Boundary measurements at a second dense scale/method and independent skin texture should determine decay rather than extending a fitted core affine map through the skin.

## Future evaluation sequence

1. Admit reliable source geometry and ownership; retain identity on degenerate or uncertain inputs.
2. Fit the internal model with independent moving correspondences, then determine support using separate decay/protection observations. Preserve accepted outliers and residual structure.
3. Evaluate the complete Jacobian and any multi-effect overlap; establish a valid inverse/coverage strategy. A safe core or positive sampled determinant alone is insufficient.
4. Solve q=F_s(p), check the inverse residual, and sample the original source once. Use the common explicit identity/failure result for unsafe support, failed inverse or uncovered targets.
5. Compare output contours, texture and protected regions against the actual endpoint mapping on the same source points.

Require a constant core vector, preserved core pairwise geometry within matching uncertainty, the observed downward sign for this endpoint order, and a correctly localized return to zero. A systematic upper/lower opposition or core distance change would favor expansion over translation.

The zero/translation/expansion/rotation controls reported at the [eye index](../README.md) qualify the measuring method only on their own synthetic cases. No actual candidate output has been rendered or accepted in this task.

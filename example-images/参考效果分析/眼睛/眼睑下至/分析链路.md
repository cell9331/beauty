# Lower-lid lowering: evidence and inference

Analysis date: 2026-10-16. Pair: `IMG_1941.PNG → IMG_1942.PNG`. Directions are forward source to target, +x right and +y down, in native screenshot photo-grid pixels. Shared input qualification and global alignment are recorded in the [eye index](../README.md). The native photo grid is retained; there is no local deformable registration and no endpoint-to-neutral split.

## Question and protected observations

The question is whether the pair changes the whole eye, the whole lower contour, or a localized outer lower-lid region. Source texture is examined separately in the outer, central, and inner thirds of each eye. The upper lid, inner corner, iris interior, brow, nose, and distant cheek are protection observations. A control name cannot establish a mechanism or a perfectly fixed anchor.

Native endpoint crops show additional opening below the outer sclera, with a much smaller visible change near the iris center and inner corner. This rules against a uniform whole-eye downward translation. It does not establish that every upper-lid or iris pixel is exactly fixed.

## Dense observation, with its limitations

The initial DIS field has positive dy concentrated on the outer lower-lid region. In the broad left/right eye windows, dy P1–P99 is approximately `0 to +8.48 px` / `0 to +11.28 px`; dx P1–P99 is `−3.08 to +1.78 px` / `−4.99 to +0.26 px`. These percentiles describe the estimated field in those windows, not anatomical maxima or independently measured contour motion.

The targeted profiles in the [evidence image](证据/位移证据.png) pass through the changed lower contour instead of only the largely stationary iris center. Both dense methods locate the changed region, but their amplitudes differ markedly. Eye-window forward/backward closure P99 is 5.02/7.21 px, and DIS versus Farneback disagreement P99 is 6.17/8.44 px. Those failures prevent a precise peak or support-boundary claim. Weak skin and nearly uniform sclera allow smooth flow estimates without providing independent texture evidence.

## Independent checks and the failed aggregate shortcut

The original source-selected 22 px grid accepted 269 of 387 candidates. Rejections were 73 low-texture, 26 ambiguous, 2 reverse-ambiguous, and 17 crop-edge cases. Of the accepted correspondences, 264 were stationary below 0.5 px, but only 4 moved more than 2 px. All-point DIS/NCC disagreement P95 was 0.10 px; **moving-point P95 was 12.33 px**. The first statistic is dominated by unchanged texture and cannot qualify the effect.

A targeted review selected 437 candidates from source texture on an 8 px spatial grid before looking at flow. The same source positions were checked with multiple template radii. NCC, peak uniqueness, and independent reverse closure were used for acceptance; optical-flow agreement was never an acceptance filter. Search started at ±64 px, expanded to ±96 px near a boundary, and remaining boundary cases were rejected.

Large templates cross a sharply changing lid/sclera boundary and can select a different edge even when correlation and reverse closure pass. Smaller templates lose uniqueness on smooth edges. The review preserves both failures; a favorable radius does not validate the others.

As a supplementary, explicitly post-review sensitivity check, 249 positions passed both 5 px and 7 px template tests with target positions within 1.5 px; 8 moved more than 2 px. Their measured dy was positive, with median 3.29 px and P5–P95 1.75–6.39 px. DIS disagreement still had P95 3.11 px. These are sparse accepted texture motions, including clusters on the inner decay of the changed region. They do not independently validate the larger dense peak. Accepted sample count is not a count of statistically independent patches because neighboring templates overlap.

## Fact → hypothesis → falsification → unknown

**Fact:** downward opening is localized to the outer lower contour, with substantial stationary inner texture. **Hypothesis:** a compact lower-lid bump with continuous surrounding support. **Excluded:** one uniform translation of the whole eye, and a requirement that the whole lower contour moves equally. **Unresolved:** peak amplitude, exact boundary, a single vertical vector versus spatially varying horizontal motion, and how the lid/iris boundary is reconstructed.

The minimal inferior-bump candidate is falsified if reliable source-selected correspondences confirm a repeatable component orthogonal to its chosen inferior axis above the local matching uncertainty. That distinction matters because the local axis itself may have a small horizontal component. A vector bump is only the next candidate, not an excuse to fit every noisy flow vector. A dense peak without unique texture cannot settle this distinction. The decisive added evidence would be traceable lower-lid texture at the peak, a higher-resolution reference, or additional endpoint/intermediate captures that preserve the same source and isolate this control.

Current evidence supports a localized downward effect and a useful failure diagnosis. It does not support a complete quantitative reconstruction.

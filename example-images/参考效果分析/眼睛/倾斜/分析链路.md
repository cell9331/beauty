# Eye tilt: evidence and inference

Analysis date: 2026-10-16. Pair: `IMG_1945.PNG → IMG_1946.PNG`. Forward source-to-target displacement uses +x right and +y down in native screenshot photo-grid pixels. Shared input identity and alignment are in the [eye index](../README.md). The common native grid is retained, without local deformable registration. A common neutral state, symmetric signed endpoints, and the intermediate response are unproven.

## Full-vector observations

Outer-side texture moves downward and inner-side texture moves upward in each eye. The two eyes have opposite image-space rotation signs. Crucially, dx changes sign between the upper and lower regions: it is not simply dy changing along the eye width. The [signed evidence and profiles](证据/位移证据.png) show both components and a return toward stationary peripheral skin.

DIS eye-window dx P1–P99 is `−5.16 to +5.48 px` / `−5.46 to +5.34 px`; dy is `−10.78 to +10.74 px` / `−10.17 to +12.52 px` for image-left/right. Forward/backward closure P95 is 0.42/0.43 px. DIS/Farneback difference P95 is 4.44/5.02 px, so skin-support contours remain method-sensitive even though both methods show the rotational pattern.

The first 22 px source grid accepted 246 of 380 candidates: 70 low-texture, 38 ambiguous, 9 reverse-ambiguous, and 17 crop-edge rejections. Among accepted points, 41 moved more than 2 px and 201 were stationary below 0.5 px. Moving DIS/NCC difference P95 was 1.26 px; stationary P95 was 0.08 px. These are method disagreements, not ground-truth errors.

## Why the original whole-window rigid fit was insufficient

An entire rectangular eye window includes the moving eye and surrounding stationary skin. Its initial rigid-fit P95 residual was 6.98/7.87 px. That statistic does not exclude a rotational eye core, because the rectangle crosses the deformation's return to identity. A rigid fit is meaningful only within a region that the source appearance identifies as a plausible moving core; the region must not be chosen by whichever points fit the desired model.

The targeted review therefore fixed a source-visible elliptical eye core before correspondence fitting, retaining the ellipse in the local [mechanism figure](证据/旋转机制复核.png). A source-only 8 px texture grid selected 410 candidates across the broader eye areas. The same positions were matched using ±64 px searches, with boundary expansion to ±96 px, NCC uniqueness and independent reverse closure. Neither optical-flow prediction nor rigid-fit residual was used to accept points.

Small templates resolve the core's changing direction more faithfully; larger templates mix texture across the rotation and support transition. More accepted points from a large template do not automatically mean a better geometric estimate. Overlapping templates also are not statistically independent trials.

The finite rotation explains both the dy slope along the eye and the dx slope across its height. A vertical-only shear can fit the first trend but misses the second, as the [independent mechanism figure](证据/旋转机制复核.png) shows. An affine transform provides only a modest residual reduction for the smaller templates; the evidence does not demand additional shear/stretch parameters in the core.

Core point-pair distances with source baselines at least 15 px provide another check. At 5 px radius, median absolute relative distance changes are 1.07%/0.89%, with P95 5.14%/4.32%. At 7 px radius, they are 1.41%/1.12%, with P95 6.21%/4.69%. This supports approximate rigidity, while retaining visible matching uncertainty. It does not establish exact distance preservation for every pixel.

## Rotation control and remaining uncertainty

A separate synthetic control rotated source eye texture by a known −8.5° with translation-free geometry. With 98 source-selected core candidates, radii 5/7/9/13 accepted 58/64/67/69 points and fitted −8.00°/−7.88°/−7.58°/−7.41°. Correspondence error P95 was 1.63/1.22/2.16/2.37 px. Radius 5 rejected 31 ambiguous, 8 reverse-ambiguous, and 1 closure case; radius 7 rejected 28 ambiguous and 6 reverse-ambiguous cases; radii 9/13 rejected 23/28 ambiguous and 8/1 reverse-ambiguous cases. This detects a template-size bias even for a genuine finite rotation. It qualifies sign and rough scale for that synthetic case; it must not be used to correct the reference angle or certify its skin transition.

**Fact:** mirrored, approximately rigid full-vector core motion fits much better than pure vertical shear. **Hypothesis:** a finite rotational eye core continuously blended to stationary surrounding skin. **Excluded as a complete core explanation:** dy-only push/pull. **Unknown:** precise angle, exact pivot, independent translation, support footprint/asymmetry, and intermediate slider response.

A rotation center is not uniquely identifiable if an independent translation is also allowed: changing the chosen center can be absorbed into translation. The fit therefore provides a rigid map, not proof of an internal pivot at the iris center. The hypothesis would be falsified by repeatable core distance changes or structured full-vector residuals substantially beyond correspondence uncertainty. New source-selected points near the support transition would distinguish a rigid core with decay from a smoothly varying local affine field.

# Eye tilt: finite rotation with continuous support

**Status: mathematical candidate only; no SDK implementation or output qualification.** The [evidence chain](分析链路.md) supports approximate core rotation over the observed endpoint span. Common coordinate, derivative, composition, inverse, and single-original-sampling rules are in [implementation conventions](../实现公共约定.md).

## Source geometry and observed-span parameterization

Freeze an input-specific source eye width `L > 0`, a convenient reference center `c`, and local support axes. `c` can be defined from reliable source geometry; it is not asserted to be the original application's pivot. Define a support `w(p) = P(p) H(t) K(n)` with a core plateau equal to one and continuous C1-or-better return to zero outside it. `t,n` are source-local coordinates normalized by `L`. The source-defined ellipse used for measurement is not a universal support mask.

In +x-right/+y-down image coordinates:

```text
R(alpha) = [[cos(alpha), −sin(alpha)],
            [sin(alpha),  cos(alpha)]]
r_s(p) = (R(s theta) − I)(p−c) + s b
F_s(p) = p + w(p) r_s(p),       0 <= s <= 1
```

Within the plateau, this is a finite rigid transform. Outside the support it is identity. `s=0` is the observed left-endpoint source, and `s=1` is the candidate right-endpoint mapping. `theta` is a pairwise angle; it is not divided into two single-endpoint angles. The reference core fits suggest roughly −8 to −9° on image-left and +8 to +9° on image-right, with template bias and incomplete support identification. These are sample observations rather than default parameters for new photos.

`b` is a fitted image-space translation. Choosing a different `c` changes the translation needed for the same rigid map; do not claim both a uniquely measured center and an independent free translation. Either keep a source-defined reference center and fit `b`, or impose and separately test a no-translation/pivot hypothesis. Finite `R` includes its horizontal component and second-order terms; replacing it by dy-only shear would discard evidence that distinguished the mechanisms.

## Complete Jacobian, including the support transition

For source geometry frozen during evaluation:

```text
A_s = R(s theta) − I
grad_w = H K grad_P + P K grad_H + P H grad_K
J_F = I + w A_s + r_s (grad_w)^T
```

`grad_H` and `grad_K` include the source-coordinate normalization and every piecewise transition derivative, as specified in the common conventions. The translation term also enters `r_s grad_w^T` at the boundary. The plateau has determinant one, but the full blended field generally does not: its boundary can compress, fold, or violate protected regions if the support is too narrow. Check the complete field over all active supports, including any overlap between eyes. Positive sampled determinant alone does not prove global injectivity.

One conservative sufficient condition for inverse fixed-point contraction is a verified bound `sup ||w A_s + r_s grad_w^T|| < 1`. This is a candidate-design budget, not something proven by the measured optical flow. Otherwise use another inverse method under the common coverage/injectivity checks. Verify `F_s(p)−q` after solving for each target `q`, then sample the original source once. A one-step `p=q−D(q)` is an approximation and cannot be silently substituted.

## Calibration and protection

Fit the finite core map to independent source-selected correspondences before fitting support. Use both dx/dy and pairwise geometry. Score stationary skin independently; including it in a single global rigid fit would force the core angle toward zero. Preserve all accepted residuals and report their spatial structure rather than rejecting points by agreement with the rotation candidate.

Support limits must be determined from decay and separate brow, nose/bridge, and cheek observations. They include following skin and therefore should not be a hard binary eye mask. The iris belongs to the rotating core in this candidate; locking its pixels while rotating the lids would be a different, unmeasured mechanism. Anisotropic or asymmetric support is a later candidate only if independent boundary evidence requires it.

Reject degenerate or unreliable eye geometry, an unsafe support transition, protected-region movement, or failed inverse/coverage checks according to the common identity/failure behavior. No model, landmark implementation, or SDK integration is selected by this document.

## Falsifiable acceptance

Compare the candidate to the observed endpoint map on the same source points. Require correct mirrored rotation signs, both vector components, approximately preserved core pairwise distances within measured matching uncertainty, a correctly located stationary transition, and actual lid/iris contour agreement. Test source-to-target and reverse correspondences and include a known finite-rotation control. Intermediate or signed neutral response requires additional references.

A coherent shear/stretch residual, a displaced iris relative to the eye core, a wrongly moving brow/bridge, or a support boundary that follows the optical-flow window rather than source texture would reject the candidate. A valid formula and safe inverse establish mathematical consistency only; they do not establish reference-effect reproduction.
